# DESIGN.md - bigTEAM Site Direction

This document captures the current design and positioning direction for
bigteam.ca. Future agents should read this before changing the homepage,
visual language, copy, or layout.

## Core Positioning

bigTEAM is a queer-led embedded co-development studio for indie games and
interactive media.

The site should communicate:

> bigTEAM is the missing part of your game team: trusted collaborators embedded
> into design meetings, grant planning, production workflows, and milestone
> delivery.

The business gap is not generic outsourcing. The gap is embedded production
capacity for small indie teams that are too serious for loose freelancers and
too small for large external-development vendors.

Primary audience:

- Funded or funding-seeking indie teams.
- Solo developers with traction.
- Small studios building a prototype, vertical slice, or funded milestone.
- Interactive media teams preparing grant, pitch, or production materials.
- Teams recovering from scope drift, staffing loss, or contractor chaos.

Use "embedded co-development" as the differentiator. Use "team extension" only
as plain-language support. Avoid leading with "staff augmentation",
"outstaffing", "freelance marketplace", or "outsourcing".

## AI Positioning

bigTEAM should be human-first.

Do not make broad AI acceleration part of the bigTEAM pitch. AI-forward work can
live under a separate personal brand or a clearly separate offer.

Safe bigTEAM stance:

> Human collaborators by default. AI-assisted work only when explicitly scoped,
> approved, disclosed, and rights-safe.

Do not turn this into a manifesto. It should be a trust signal, not the whole
brand.

## Brand Feeling

The design should feel like:

- A blacklight indie game production toybox.
- A loud club poster.
- A playful production board.
- A tactile 3D desk full of connected game-making objects.
- A team that is weird, fun, senior, and production-literate.

Keywords:

- embedded
- playful
- maximalist
- trusted
- tactile
- queer
- production-aware
- grant-literate
- indie-native
- human-first

Avoid:

- corporate SaaS
- minimalist consultant sites
- generic agency templates
- AI startup aesthetics
- stock art
- muted beige professionalism
- soft pastel-only agency styling
- full-page direct homage to any specific anime or game property

## Visual References

Useful reference directions:

- charm.land: soft matte 3D, toy-like technical objects, playful confidence.
- kinioto-anime.com: bold first impression, high contrast, pop energy.

Important distinction:

- Acid green can be used as an accent, but avoid making the whole site a direct
  "green yuri" visual reference. The brand should be ownable: ultraviolet club
  palette plus arcade/production objects, not lime anime key art.

## Visual World

Use a recurring world of toy-like production objects:

- milestone cubes
- design/code/art/production pods
- cables and plugs
- sticky notes
- grant forms
- pitch decks
- tiny game windows
- controllers
- checklists
- calendar blocks
- cursor arrows
- status lights
- portfolio cards
- handwritten callouts
- tape labels and stickers

The main hero graphic may change, but the object language should remain
consistent. The visual metaphor is:

> Game teams, grant plans, milestones, and collaborators are physically plugged
> together.

## Palette

Use a blacklight club palette with warm readable surfaces.

```text
Ink Black      #101016
Deep Violet    #24113F
Ultraviolet    #6D3CFF
Club Purple    #9B5CFF
Hot Pink       #FF4FA3
Acid Green     #B8FF00
Pool Cyan      #4DE7FF
Warm Paper     #FFF4E6
Soft Lavender  #E2CFFF
Coral          #FF7D6E
```

Usage rules:

- Dark violet/black should dominate hero and high-impact sections.
- Warm paper and soft lavender should hold readable content.
- Hot pink is the primary CTA/action color.
- Acid green is for callouts, status stickers, arrows, and labels. Do not let it
  dominate the full page.
- Cyan is for secondary glow, cables, hover states, and small highlights.

## Typography

Use three typographic voices:

1. Display headline: chunky, loud, poster-like, used for hero and major section
   statements.
2. Body sans: clean, warm, readable, and not corporate.
3. Mono/label font: used for production tags and stickers.

Example labels:

```text
GRANT-TO-SLICE
MILESTONE
DESIGN ROOM
SCOPE CHECK
PRODUCTION POD
WE JOIN THE ROOM
NO HANDOFF WEIRDNESS
```

Do not rely on negative letter spacing. Keep responsive type legible and avoid
text overflow on mobile.

## Copy Voice

The voice should be playful, direct, and operationally serious.

Good:

> We join the room.

> The missing part of your game team.

> Embedded collaborators for grants, vertical slices, and milestone delivery.

> Freelancers can do tasks. Outsourcing can ship handoffs. bigTEAM embeds
> trusted collaborators into the project.

Avoid vague agency copy:

- We make dreams come true.
- We are passionate creators.
- We assemble indie talent.
- Diverse teams help you qualify for grants.

Identity and queerness should signal trust, community, and safer collaboration,
not transactional grant positioning.

## Homepage Structure

The homepage should be a pitch, not a brochure.

1. Hero
   - Big positioning statement.
   - Busy production-toybox graphic.
   - CTA.
   - Bottom split proof bar.

2. Not Outsourcing
   - Loud contrast section explaining that bigTEAM embeds collaborators into the
     actual project context.

3. Embedded Pods
   - Three primary offers:
     - Grant-to-Slice Pod
     - Milestone Team Extension
     - Project Rescue

4. How We Embed
   - Visual process board:
     `scope -> team -> meetings -> build -> milestone -> pitch`

5. Inside The Room
   - Graphical callout grid:
     - design meetings
     - backlog shaping
     - grant/pitch support
     - production planning
     - creative review
     - milestone delivery

6. Selected Work
   - Case-study cards, not a vague dump.
   - Each card should show:
     - project
     - role
     - what bigTEAM contributed
     - link

7. Human-First Trust
   - Short section on human collaboration, rights-aware production, transparent
     process, and AI only when explicitly scoped.

8. CTA
   - Make it feel like submitting a mission/project brief.
   - Suggested CTA: "Tell us about your next milestone."

## Hero Direction

Preferred hero headline:

> The missing part of your game team.

Preferred subhead:

> Embedded collaborators for grants, vertical slices, and milestone delivery.

Alternate headlines:

- We join the room.
- Embedded co-dev for indie teams with real deadlines.
- Your next milestone needs more than tickets.

Desktop hero layout:

```text
[logo/nav/contact]

[large headline]          [maximalist connected production scene]
[subhead]
[CTA] [secondary link]

[bottom split bar: trust chips | portfolio thumbnails]
```

The hero graphic should be busy but anchored:

- One main headline area.
- One central object, usually a `MILESTONE` cube.
- Four secondary nodes: `DESIGN`, `CODE`, `ART`, `PRODUCTION`.
- Cables, arrows, sticky notes, and labels should add energy without replacing
  the content.

## Bottom Split Bar

Keep the split proof bar idea. It gives immediate credibility and helps justify
the maximalist hero.

Desktop:

- Left half: trust/proof chips.
- Right half: portfolio micro-cards.

Trust chips:

- Human-first collaboration
- Rights-aware production
- Trusted network
- Embedded in your tools

Portfolio micro-cards:

- Mer-makin it!
- Date or Mate
- Cosmic Cruiser Repair
- More work

Tablet:

- Split bar becomes two stacked strips.
- Trust chips can be horizontally scrollable.
- Portfolio cards can be horizontally scrollable.

Mobile:

- Compress into micro carousels or auto-advancing button rails.
- One rail for trust callouts.
- One rail for portfolio cards.

## Graphical Callouts

Use callouts heavily. They are part of the maximalist system and worked well in
mobile concepts.

Callout forms:

- sticky notes
- tape labels
- arrow stickers
- tooltip bubbles
- stamp badges
- mini checklists
- status pills

Callout phrases:

- WE JOIN THE ROOM
- NO HANDOFF WEIRDNESS
- GRANT READY
- SCOPE BEFORE CHAOS
- HUMANS IN THE LOOP
- DESIGN CALLS INCLUDED
- BUILD WITH CONTEXT
- MILESTONE READY

Callouts should feel like stickers slapped onto the layout.

## Responsive Design Rules

Do not shrink the desktop poster into mobile. Recompose by breakpoint.

Desktop:

- Full maximal scene.
- Split bottom bar.
- Layered callouts.
- Portfolio visible immediately.
- Wide hero can use a two-column grid.

Tablet:

- Hero text moves above or beside a cropped scene.
- The production scene can bleed offscreen.
- Split bar becomes stacked strips.
- Callouts wrap into dense grids.

Mobile:

- Text first.
- CTA above the fold.
- Graphic becomes vertical/cropped and simplified.
- Central `MILESTONE` object remains visible.
- Trust and portfolio become micro carousels.
- Service cards swipe horizontally.
- Decorative extras disappear before important labels.

Mobile structure:

```text
bigTEAM        [contact]

The missing part
of your game team.

Embedded collaborators for grants,
vertical slices, and milestone delivery.

[Tell us about your milestone]

       DESIGN
         |
ART -- MILESTONE -- CODE
         |
    PRODUCTION

[Grant-to-Slice] [Team Extension] [Project Rescue]

[Human-first] [Rights-aware] [Trusted network]
[Mer-makin it!] [Date or Mate] [Cosmic Cruiser]
```

## Motion

Motion should be playful but not block content.

Good motion:

- cables pulse
- callouts wiggle once on entry
- portfolio rail auto-scrolls slowly
- sticky notes float 2-4px
- CTA has punchy hover
- status lights blink rarely

Avoid:

- scroll-jacking
- loading gates
- motion that is needed to understand the page
- long intro animations

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Implementation Guidance

Build the site from layered components, not a single giant image.

Important text must be HTML, not baked into generated artwork.

Recommended implementation:

- Use Astro components when the site grows beyond one page.
- Keep content data for work cards/services in structured arrays.
- Use generated PNG/WebP assets for 3D objects and backgrounds.
- Use CSS/SVG/HTML for stickers, labels, buttons, and callouts when possible.
- Use separate art crops per breakpoint with `<picture>` when a large hero image
  is used.
- Keep all links accessible and all text readable without images.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold media.
- Optimize image sizes before committing.

Breakpoint assets:

- Desktop hero: wide `16:9` or wider.
- Tablet hero: `4:3` or portrait crop.
- Mobile hero: `4:5` or `9:16`, recomposed around the milestone object.
